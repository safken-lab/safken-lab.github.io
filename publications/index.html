  • Variational Inference for Uncertain Optimal Transport via Sinkhorn Parametrization

    Ananyapam De, Linus Bleistein, Anton Frederik Thielmann, Benjamin Säfken

    ICML · 2026

    Abstract summary

    Optimal transport estimates how to match two distributions, for example by assigning mass from one set of points to another. When the matching costs are uncertain, several transport plans may be plausible. We turn the Sinkhorn algorithm into a differentiable map from unconstrained samples to entropy-regularized transport plans. This lets a normalizing flow learn a distribution over plans while every sample still obeys the required row and column sums.

    In our experiments, the variational approximation recovered distributions similar to those produced by more expensive sampling methods. With these computational savings, we could apply the method to larger problems, where repeated posterior sampling can become impractical.

  • Beyond Black-Box Predictions: Identifying Marginal Feature Effects in Tabular Transformer Networks

    Anton Frederik Thielmann, Arik Reuter, Benjamin Säfken

    AISTATS · 2026

    Abstract summary

    Tabular transformers learn interactions between input features, which makes it difficult to read off the effect of a single variable. NAMformer uses a transformer for prediction and an additive representation for the marginal effects. A key design choice is to retain feature embeddings before the transformer mixes information across columns, so the model has a representation from which it can estimate individual effects.

    We give conditions under which these effects can be identified and use ablation experiments to test whether the architecture recovers them when interactions are present. On our prediction benchmarks, the model can reach the performance of black-box alternatives while providing effect curves that a reader can inspect.

  • From Uniform to Learned Knots: A Study of Spline-Based Numerical Encodings for Tabular Deep Learning

    Manish Kumar, Anton Frederik Thielmann, Christoph Weisser, Benjamin Säfken

    Transactions on Machine Learning Research · 2026

    Abstract summary

    We ask how a neural network should represent a continuous tabular feature before learning from it. We compare piecewise-linear encodings with B-splines, M-splines, and integrated splines across multilayer perceptrons, residual networks, and tabular transformers. We also compare knots placed on an even grid, at data quantiles, using target information, or learned jointly with model weights.

    Piecewise-linear encodings were a strong default for classification. In regression, the best encoding depended on the backbone, basis, knot placement, and resolution. Our parameterization let us learn knot locations stably, but added training cost. Our resolution experiments also showed that adding more basis functions often stopped helping beyond a moderate size.

  • EviNAM: Intelligibility and Uncertainty via Evidential Neural Additive Models

    Sören Schleibaum, Anton Frederik Thielmann, Julian Teusch, Benjamin Säfken, Jörg P. Müller

    arXiv preprint · 2026

    Abstract summary

    EviNAM combines neural additive models with evidential learning to show which features influence a prediction and why that prediction may be uncertain. It estimates data noise and uncertainty about the model separately, alongside each feature’s contribution. These quantities are available in one forward pass at inference time, which avoids repeated sampling or evaluation of multiple fitted models.

    We compare EviNAM with additive baselines on synthetic and real datasets. It achieves similar predictive performance to several baselines for regression in our experiments. For classification, it can express predicted class probabilities as feature contributions. We use a housing example to illustrate how the feature-level uncertainty estimates can vary across the predictor range.

  • Quality Without Usefulness: LLM-Generated XAI Narratives as Trust Heuristics Rather Than Decision Aids

    Fabian Lukassen, Jan Herrmann, Christoph Weisser, Alexander Silbersdorff, Benjamin Säfken, Thomas Kneib

    arXiv preprint · 2026

    Abstract summary

    Can a clear explanation of a forecast improve the decision that follows? We study that question in energy forecasting through five controlled experiments, covering 2,730 judgments across 60 test instances. We keep explanation quality high and evaluate whether explanations improve performance on tasks such as recognizing unreliable forecasts.

    The explanations failed to improve accuracy on the five tasks and raised self-reported confidence. A placebo control attributed that increase to the presence of text. In an out-of-distribution detection task, the narratives made the language-model judge less likely to flag unreliable predictions. We argue for evaluating explanation systems by the decisions they support as well as the quality of their prose.

  • LLM-Augmented Changepoint Detection: A Framework for Ensemble Detection and Automated Explanation

    Fabian Lukassen, Christoph Weisser, Michael Schlee, Manish Kumar, Anton Thielmann, Benjamin Säfken, Alexander Silbersdorff, Thomas Kneib

    arXiv preprint · 2026

    Abstract summary

    Changepoint detectors look for shifts in the behaviour of a time series. Different detectors respond to different kinds of shifts, so choosing a single method in advance can be difficult. Our system combines the outputs of ten statistical detectors. A separate language-model stage writes explanations that relate the detected changes to potentially relevant events.

    For private or specialized datasets, users can supply documents that the system retrieves when composing an explanation. We report more robust detection from the ensemble and illustrate the workflow in finance, political science, and environmental science. The explanations provide possible context for a detected change, which analysts can investigate further.

  • From XAI to Stories: A Factorial Study of LLM-Generated Explanation Quality

    Fabian Lukassen, Jan Herrmann, Christoph Weisser, Benjamin Säfken, Thomas Kneib

    arXiv preprint · 2026

    Abstract summary

    We test how to turn numerical explanations of time-series forecasts into readable prose. We vary four forecasting models, three feature-attribution conditions including a baseline without attributions, three language models, and eight prompting strategies. Two language-model judges score 660 explanations against four quality criteria.

    In this experiment, the choice of language model had the largest influence on the scores. SHAP or LIME gave small gains over the baseline for expert-oriented explanations. Zero-shot prompts were competitive with more expensive self-consistency prompts, and chain-of-thought prompting lowered scores. We measure explanation quality as judged by the evaluators, leaving downstream decision usefulness as a separate question.

  • Penalized regression splines in Mixture Density Networks

    Quentin Edward Seifert, Anton Thielmann, Elisabeth Bergherr, Benjamin Säfken, Jakob Zierk, Manfred Rauh, Tobias Hepp

    The International Journal of Biostatistics · 2025

    Abstract summary

    A mixture density network predicts the parameters of several component distributions, whose combination can describe a skewed or multimodal response. As covariates change, mixture components can exchange roles, which makes their fitted parameter curves difficult to interpret. We use penalized spline basis functions to model how mixture parameters vary across the predictor range.

    We compare this design with a conventional network in simulations and apply it to laboratory reference data. The spline penalties control the smoothness of the parameter curves, giving the analyst a way to inspect how the conditional distribution changes with a covariate.

  • Probabilistic Topic Modeling With Transformer Representations

    Arik Reuter, Anton Thielmann, Christoph Weisser, Benjamin Säfken, Thomas Kneib

    IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems · 2025

    Abstract summary

    TNTM combines two approaches to topic modelling. Transformer embeddings capture words in context, while a probabilistic model represents a document as a mixture of topics. A variational autoencoder estimates the topic model efficiently, using the transformer representations to help define coherent topics.

    We compare the method with existing models using topic coherence in embedding space and diversity across topics. In our experiments, TNTM produced competitive coherence and very high diversity. This is useful when fitting many topics, where a model can otherwise return several topics that describe nearly the same subject.

  • NODE-GAMLSS: Interpretable Uncertainty Modelling via Deep Distributional Regression

    Ananyapam De, Anton Frederik Thielmann, Benjamin Säfken

    NeurIPS Workshop on Bayesian Decision-making and Uncertainty · 2024

    Abstract summary

    NODE-GAMLSS models the conditional distribution of a response using additive ensembles of differentiable oblivious decision trees. Each feature can contribute to parameters such as location, scale, and shape. The model learns those parameters jointly through the likelihood, which makes it possible to estimate conditional quantiles and inspect individual feature effects.

    We compare NODE-GAMLSS with interpretable distributional regression methods on simulated and real datasets. Our experiments illustrate its ability to recover changing quantiles and display how predictors influence the fitted distribution. This is especially useful when variability or tail behaviour changes across observations.

  • Neural Additive Models for Location Scale and Shape: A Framework for Interpretable Neural Regression Beyond the Mean

    Anton Frederik Thielmann, René-Marcel Kruse, Thomas Kneib, Benjamin Säfken

    AISTATS · 2024

    Abstract summary

    An ordinary neural additive model assigns each predictor a small neural network and adds the outputs to predict a mean. NAMLSS extends that design to a chosen response distribution, with additive feature effects for its parameters. A feature can therefore influence the expected outcome, the spread, or another aspect of the distribution through different fitted curves.

    We describe how to train the model using the likelihood and illustrate the architecture with simulated data from a flexible Johnson distribution. The examples show how the fitted effects can reveal changes in spread or shape that a mean prediction alone would miss.

  • Mambular: A Sequential Model for Tabular Deep Learning

    Anton Frederik Thielmann, Manish Kumar, Christoph Weisser, Arik Reuter, Benjamin Säfken, Soheila Samiee

    arXiv preprint · 2024

    Abstract summary

    Mambular treats a table row as a sequence of feature embeddings and applies structured state-space layers to that sequence. These layers pass a learned state between features so that a prediction can reflect relationships among columns. We test design choices such as pooling the feature outputs, processing features in both directions, and changing how features interact.

    We benchmark the architecture against established tabular predictors. Our results show that sequence-based state-space models can work well on tabular tasks, and the comparisons help clarify which architectural choices contribute to that performance.

  • STREAM: Simplified Topic Retrieval, Exploration, and Analysis Module

    Anton Frederik Thielmann, Arik Reuter, Benjamin Säfken, Christoph Weisser, Manish Kumar, Gillian Kant

    ACL · 2024

    Abstract summary

    STREAM brings topic modelling, evaluation, and interactive exploration into a single Python workflow. It supports inspecting topic representations, comparing relationships between topics, and studying how topics relate to other variables. We include an option to use topic information in downstream interpretable models alongside ordinary tabular features.

    We add evaluation measures based on an intruder-word task, which tests whether an unrelated word can be distinguished from the words representing a topic. We also introduce benchmark datasets to support more systematic comparisons between topic models.

  • GPTopic: Dynamic and Interactive Topic Representations

    Arik Reuter, Bishnu Khadka, Anton Thielmann, Christoph Weisser, Sebastian Fischer, Benjamin Säfken

    arXiv preprint · 2024

    Abstract summary

    Topic models often describe a topic with a short list of frequent or distinctive words. GPTopic uses a language model to produce a fuller description from the evidence associated with a topic, such as representative documents and top words. A chat interface lets a reader ask questions about that evidence or refine the topic description as their analysis develops.

    We present the workflow and show how a user can move from an initial overview to more targeted questions or revisions. We provide a way to explore and explain topics interactively after fitting a topic model.

  • AStA

    Editorial special issue: Bridging the gap between AI and Statistics

    Benjamin Säfken, David Rügamer

    AStA Advances in Statistical Analysis · 2024

    Editorial overview

    We introduce a special issue about how statistical ideas can help explain, evaluate, and extend modern machine learning. We place the contributions in the context of shared problems such as understanding fitted models, quantifying uncertainty, and making complex models easier to train or use.

    We highlight work on conditional feature importance, uncertainty in additive models, variational inference with flexible posteriors, and efficient hyperparameter tuning. We also cover applications such as distributional regression and modelling relationships between topics and document metadata.

  • Rage Against the Mean – A Review of Distributional Regression Approaches

    Thomas Kneib, Alexander Silbersdorff, Benjamin Säfken

    Econometrics and Statistics · 2023

    Abstract summary

    Two groups can have similar average outcomes while differing in variability, lower-tail risk, or the probability of extreme values. We survey regression methods that let those features of an outcome distribution depend on covariates. We relate quantile and expectile regression to models that specify several distribution parameters or model a conditional distribution more directly.

    We discuss how model choice depends on the scientific question and what can be learned from different distributional targets. Examples include analysing tail risk and studying how predictor effects change across the outcome distribution. We also discuss how to interpret the resulting effects.

  • Conditional Model Selection in Mixed-Effects Models with cAIC4

    Benjamin Säfken, David Rügamer, Thomas Kneib, Sonja Greven

    Journal of Statistical Software · 2021

    Abstract summary

    Mixed-effects models can predict new measurements for subjects or groups that have already been observed. Conditional AIC evaluates models for that prediction setting by accounting for the fitted random effects. Estimating their variance introduces additional uncertainty, which affects the penalty used to compare models.

    We describe the cAIC4 R package, show how to compute the criterion for fitted linear and generalized mixed models and additive mixed models, and demonstrate stepwise selection. The examples show how the criterion can guide choices about model terms and random-effect structure in practice.

  • Teaching Statistics

    Introductory data science across disciplines, using Python, case studies, and industry consulting projects

    Johanna Lasser, Debsankha Manik, Alexander Silbersdorff, Benjamin Säfken, Thomas Kneib

    Teaching Statistics · 2021

    Abstract summary

    We describe an introductory data science course at the University of Göttingen for students from multiple disciplines, including students new to programming and statistics. It starts with Python and data handling, moves through analysis and visualization in discipline-specific case studies, and finishes with a project using a real dataset.

    We developed some projects with industry and research partners so students could practise cleaning data, fitting and interpreting a basic statistical model, and communicating findings in context. We explain the course structure, point to openly available teaching materials, and discuss early evaluations.

  • TTLocVis: A Twitter Topic Location Visualization Package

    Gillian Kant, Christoph Weisser, Benjamin Säfken

    Journal of Open Source Software · 2020

    Abstract summary

    TTLocVis is a Python package for studying how topics in geolocated tweets vary across places and over time. Individual tweets are short, so the workflow can pool tweets by hashtag before fitting latent Dirichlet allocation. The package helps with preparing the text, fitting topic models, and associating the resulting topic estimates with geographic information.

    Analysts can examine the output through maps, word clouds, and time series of topic prevalence. These views help connect themes in the text with when and where they appear. We describe the software workflow and the types of analysis it supports.

  • Smoothing Parameter and Model Selection for General Smooth Models

    Simon N. Wood, Natalya Pya, Benjamin Säfken

    Journal of the American Statistical Association · 2016

    Abstract summary

    We develop a common fitting framework for models whose predictors contain smooth functions. We represent the functions with spline-like bases and use quadratic penalties to control their complexity. We estimate the smoothing parameters using a Laplace approximation to the marginal likelihood, allowing the same approach to handle a range of likelihoods.

    The framework quantifies uncertainty about the smoothing parameters and uses it to improve Akaike-based model selection. The examples cover generalized additive, distributional, survival, and multivariate models. We also show how supplying derivatives of a new likelihood can extend the framework to additional model families.

  • A unifying approach to the estimation of the conditional Akaike information in generalized linear mixed models

    Benjamin Säfken, Thomas Kneib, Clara-Sophie van Waveren, Sonja Greven

    Electronic Journal of Statistics · 2014

    Abstract summary

    Conditional AIC compares mixed-effects models when the goal is prediction for clusters whose random effects have already been estimated. We develop a common approach to estimating the optimism correction when the random-effects covariance is itself fitted. This extends conditional model selection to generalized mixed-effects models with several response distributions.

    We recover the Gaussian case within the framework and derive criteria for Poisson and exponential responses. We discuss where the same derivation is unavailable and use simulation studies to examine how the estimators behave. The result helps distinguish which model comparison is appropriate for a given prediction target.
